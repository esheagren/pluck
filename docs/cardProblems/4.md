# Misinterpreted Core Concept

## Original Card

**Question:** Why might deliberately making mistakes be effective for learning that transfers to new situations?

**Answer:** Making mistakes forces learners to confront their misconceptions and develop deeper understanding of underlying principles, which enables better application to novel contexts

---

## Problems Identified

- **Concept distortion:** The source material describes "error-based learning" where trainees deliberately seek out *situations* in which they make mistakes (i.e., challenging scenarios where errors naturally occur). The card reframes this as "deliberately making mistakes" - as if intentionally getting something wrong is the technique.

- **Logical incoherence:** If someone deliberately makes a mistake (intentionally answers wrong), they aren't actually developing the skills needed to understand the concept. You can't learn to solve a problem by purposely solving it incorrectly.

- **Answer doesn't rescue the question:** The answer talks about "confronting misconceptions," which partially fits the wrong interpretation but doesn't capture the actual principle of seeking challenging learning situations.

---

## Reconstructed Cards

### Card 1: Error-Based Learning Definition

> **Q:** What is error-based learning?
>
> **A:** A training approach where learners deliberately seek out challenging situations where they're likely to make mistakes, rather than practicing only what they already know

**Why this is better:** Accurately captures that learners seek *situations*, not errors themselves.

### Card 2: Why Error-Based Learning Works

> **Q:** Why does deliberately practicing in situations where you're likely to make mistakes improve learning transfer?
>
> **A:** Encountering and working through natural errors in challenging situations builds deeper understanding of underlying principles, enabling better application to novel contexts

**Why this is better:** Makes clear the mechanism - you encounter errors naturally through challenge, not by intentionally performing incorrectly.

---

## Key Principle

Preserve the logical structure of the source concept—rephrasing should not change *what* is deliberate (seeking challenge vs. making errors) in a way that renders the idea incoherent.
